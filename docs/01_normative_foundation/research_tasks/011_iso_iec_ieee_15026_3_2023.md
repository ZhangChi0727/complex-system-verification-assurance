## Objective

Study system integrity levels as a possible generic assurance-intensity mechanism, use the reviewed IEEE 1012 source-native model, and own the final non-equivalence comparison separating Part 3 from IEEE and domain-specific DAL/FDAL/IDAL regimes.

## Required questions

- How are integrity levels defined, assigned, used and maintained?
- What claims, evidence or process rigor are related to integrity levels?
- Which relations can support Assurance Applicability/Rigor without imposing a universal scale?
- How does the model compare with IEEE 1012 and aviation levels, and where is equivalence prohibited?

## Required work and outputs

Verify source identity; produce `../standard_notes/iso_iec_ieee_15026_3_2023_clause_study.md`; create cross-standard level/rigor mapping with explicit non-equivalence; update ISO-G03B/ARP-G01 candidate scope; record impact and independent review.

## Stop conditions

Do not define a universal integrity algorithm, equate named level schemes or infer certification/legal authority.

## Research contribution contract

This task answers RQ3/RQ4/RQ5 through complete analysis of 4.4 level claims, 4.5 requirements/justification, 4.6 determination process, Clauses 5–8 use/determination/allocation/satisfaction and Clause 9 agreement/approval. It shall determine whether an integrity level is a property, claim, classification or control input rather than assuming it is merely assurance intensity.

## Candidate falsification tests

Test `INN-T1/A1` against explicit source semantics for level justification, satisfaction, agreement and approval. Record whether an equivalent generic sufficiency/rigor interface exists, or whether the source only constrains a particular level scheme.

## Negative findings and non-answers

Do not invent a universal scale, numerical crosswalk or equality with IEEE/DAL schemes. Absence of a common scale is `NO EVIDENCE`, not novelty.

## Generalization rights

Level reasoning primitives may be `Extension`; a specific scheme remains `Profile`; project thresholds remain `Practice`; only scheme-independent reviewed relations may be proposed for `Generic`.

## Synthesis handoff dataset

Emit the common record plus `level_role`, `claim_requirement_justification`, `determination_allocation_satisfaction`, `agreement_approval`, `cross_scheme_relation` and `non_equivalence`.

## Detailed execution specification

Produce a clause-level system-integrity-level model using the reviewed Part 1:2025 vocabulary. The work shall test whether the model can provide a generic assurance-intensity hook while preserving domain-specific scales and authorities.

### Source gate and inventory

Confirm path, edition/date, 32-page extent, SHA-256 and completeness. Inventory scope, normative references, terms, all substantive clauses, figures/tables and annexes. Record how Part 3 depends on Part 1 and whether cited editions require qualification.

### Mandatory research packages

Analyse 4.4 level claim, 4.5 requirements and justification, 4.6 determination process, Clauses 5–8 use/determination/allocation/satisfaction, and Clause 9 agreement/approval authority. Determine the definition and purpose of system integrity levels; entities to which a level applies; establishment/assignment inputs and decision responsibility; consequence, risk, confidence or other bases actually used; relation to claims, assurance objectives, evidence and process rigor; allocation/decomposition/composition; maintenance under lifecycle change; communication/documentation; and tailoring/context limitations. Classify each level occurrence as property, claim, classification, control input or `not determined`.

### Clause evidence record

For each proposition capture exact locator/PDF page, modality/source class, native object/relation, inputs and decision criteria, responsible role/authority if specified, output/information item, lifecycle/change condition, VAF implication and confidence. Separate a source-defined level from the framework's generic `Assurance Applicability/Rigor` abstraction.
