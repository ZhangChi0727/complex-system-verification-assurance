# ISO/IEC/IEEE 15288:2023 Normative Research Note

## 1. Research scope and reading rules

This note records a clause-level study of ISO/IEC/IEEE 15288:2023, *Systems and software engineering — System life cycle processes*, second edition, May 2023. It covers Clauses 3, 4, 5.6–5.8, 5.10, 6.3.1–6.3.8, 6.4.2–6.4.3, 6.4.9, 6.4.11, Annex B, and Annex D.

The licensed PDF is a research input only and is not part of the repository. This note paraphrases the source and preserves clause locators; it does not reproduce standard text.

The standard supplies a common process framework for human-made systems and their life cycles, including application to a system of interest, system elements, and systems of systems. It does not prescribe a particular life-cycle model, development method, modeling approach, or verification technique. Consequently, this study uses it as a product-independent process foundation rather than as a complete assurance regime.

### Classification legend

| Label | Meaning in this repository |
|---|---|
| **NORMATIVE** | Directly supported by a requirement, permission, outcome, purpose, or definition in the normative body, with conformance context stated where necessary. |
| **INFORMATIVE** | Supported by a NOTE, example, or informative annex; useful guidance but not independently mandatory. |
| **INTERPRETATION** | A reasoned reading that connects source statements without presenting the connection as source wording. |
| **FRAMEWORK IMPLICATION** | A required change or constraint on the Verification Assurance Framework derived from the study. |
| **RESEARCH PROPOSAL** | A framework construct not established by this standard and requiring later justification. |

### Conformance control

Clause 4 permits full conformance by declared outcomes or by declared tasks, and tailored conformance through Annex A. Under outcome-based conformance, Clause 6 activities and tasks are guidance regardless of their modal verb. Under task-based conformance, the activities and tasks are the requirements and the outcomes are guidance. Therefore, a statement such as “the standard requires task X” is incomplete unless the selected conformance mode is stated. In this note, **direct normative support** means that the concept is present in the normative body; it does not mean that every adopter must perform every task under every conformance claim.

### 2.1 Requirement validation, verification, and verifiability analysis

The standard separates three related concerns that the framework must not collapse:

| Concern | Clause support | Classification | Framework consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stakeholder requirement validation | 6.4.2.3(e)(3) directly requires feeding analyzed requirements back to applicable stakeholders to validate that needs and expectations were captured and expressed adequately. | **NORMATIVE**, conformance-qualified | Treat stakeholder confirmation as a direct function, distinct from system/product validation. |
| System requirement validation | 6.4.3.3(c)(3) directly requires stakeholder review; NOTE 18 explains this as an application of Validation to the requirements. | Review task **NORMATIVE**; validation interpretation **INFORMATIVE** | Preserve the stakeholder-needs reference and do not equate this automatically with the full 6.4.11 system Validation Process. |
| Requirement verification / quality analysis | 6.4.2.3(e)(1) and 6.4.3.3(c)(1) directly require analysis of the complete requirement sets. The associated NOTEs list candidate characteristics, and 6.4.3.3(b) NOTE 14 relates Verification to good-requirement quality. | Analysis tasks **NORMATIVE**; characteristics and V&V relationship **INFORMATIVE** | V2 is directly supported in function, while its name and boundary as an independent Verification Activity remain framework-defined. |

For stakeholder requirements, the NOTE examples address individual qualities such as necessity, implementation independence, ambiguity, completeness, singularity, achievability, verifiability, and conformance, plus set-level completeness, consistency, feasibility/affordability, and boundedness. For system requirements, the NOTE examples include necessity, implementation freedom, ambiguity, consistency, completeness, singularity, feasibility, traceability, verifiability, affordability, and boundedness. These lists are informative guidance, not a new repository-authored normative checklist.

## 3. Process architecture and assurance context

### 3.1 Process description — Clauses 5.6 and 5.7

- **Source classification:** **NORMATIVE** body.
- **Intent:** define a common process description structure and prevent the document order from being mistaken for execution order.
- **Objects:** process title, purpose, outcomes, activities, tasks, and optional outputs.
- **Outputs:** outputs may be artefacts or information items; Annex B provides non-exhaustive examples.
- **Relationship:** purpose and outcomes describe what the process achieves; activities and tasks describe contributing work.
- **FRAMEWORK IMPLICATION:** every V-activity record should identify its parent process contribution, intended outcomes, tasks, and information outputs. The numerical V0–V12 order is an identifier scheme, not a standards-derived temporal sequence.

### 3.2 Application, iteration, recursion, concurrency, and process views — Clause 5.8

- **Source classification:** **NORMATIVE** body, with explanatory **INFORMATIVE** notes.
- **Intent:** permit life-cycle processes to be invoked as needed and applied iteratively, recursively, and concurrently.
- **Objects:** life-cycle processes, system levels, iterations, process views.
- **Relationship:** repeated verification and integration can build confidence incrementally; process views can combine activities and tasks from multiple processes without creating new tasks.
- **FRAMEWORK IMPLICATION:** V0–V12 shall be described as a **Verification Activity Architecture / process view**. Activities may recur, overlap, or be entered in response to change, risk, anomaly, or evidence needs. Gates are project-control decisions, not proof of a universal waterfall.
- **Open question:** later standards may impose domain-specific sequencing or transition criteria.

### 3.3 Assurance and assurance cases — Clause 5.10

- **Source classification:** assurance definitions and discussion occur in the normative body; integrating assurance across the life cycle is expressed as a recommendation.
- **Intent:** explain how justified confidence in a claim can be established.
- **Objects:** claim, context, argument, evidence, subclaims, and reasoning.
- **Outputs:** an auditable assurance case; evidence may include pass/fail results, measurements, or qualitative evaluations.
- **Relationship:** evidence often does not directly justify a top-level claim; structured reasoning and subclaims bridge the gap. An assurance case may reveal requirements-coverage gaps.
- **FRAMEWORK IMPLICATION:** `Claim → Argument → Evidence` has direct conceptual support. Traceability is necessary for navigation and audit but is not a substitute for argument or sufficiency reasoning.
- **Boundary:** the term **Verification Assurance** and a universal Verification Sufficiency calculus remain **RESEARCH PROPOSAL** constructs.

## 4. Verification Process — Clause 6.4.9

### 4.1 Purpose and outcomes — 6.4.9.1–6.4.9.2

- **Source classification:** **NORMATIVE**.
- **Intent:** obtain objective evidence that a system, system element, or artefact fulfils specified requirements and characteristics, while detecting anomalies in life-cycle artefacts.
- **Objects:** requirements, architecture, design, implemented system elements, life-cycle process artefacts, and verification results.
- **Outcomes:** constraints and enablers are identified; the object is verified; corrective-action data and objective evidence are produced; results and anomalies are identified; traceability is established.
- **Relationships:** verification refers to requirements and technical descriptions; anomaly resolution invokes project assessment/control and other technical processes; configuration management controls baselines.
- **FRAMEWORK IMPLICATION:** the framework must support verification of descriptions and models, not only the integrated product, and must distinguish result, anomaly, corrective action, evidence, and approval.

### 4.2 Prepare for verification — 6.4.9.3(a)

- **Source classification:** tasks are **NORMATIVE** under task-based conformance; associated NOTE details are **INFORMATIVE**.
- **Inputs/objects:** candidate objects, requirements and characteristics, project constraints, risks, architecture/design, available enablers.
- **Tasks:** identify scope and actions; identify constraints; select methods and success criteria for each action; define a verification strategy; feed verification constraints/objectives back into requirements, architecture, and design; plan and obtain enabling systems or services.
- **Outputs:** defined scope/actions, selected methods and criteria, verification strategy, enabler plans/access, and design feedback.
- **Relationships:** project planning coordinates the work; decision management supports trade-offs; risk management informs selection and omission; validation confirms an enabling system is suitable for intended use.
- **FRAMEWORK IMPLICATION:** Verification Strategy is promoted from a repository-only proposal to a normatively supported concept. However, the repository's complete VSR field set is not thereby mandated.

The method examples in the associated NOTE form a useful first-level taxonomy:

| Method | Source treatment | Framework treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Inspection | Informative method example; peer review is an inspection example. | Retain as a method. Do not make “review” a separate peer of inspection without another source. |
| Analysis | Informative method example, including modeling, simulation, analogy, and similarity. | Retain as a method; techniques remain separately classified. |
| Demonstration | Informative method example. | Add as a method candidate. |
| Testing | Informative method example. | Retain as one method, not a synonym for verification. |

The same NOTE says method selection may consider the item, system, and project risk. This supports risk-informed selection but does not prescribe a risk-to-method algorithm.

### 4.3 Verification strategy — 6.4.9.3(a)(4)

- **Source classification:** defining the strategy is **NORMATIVE** under task-based conformance; the NOTE's detailed contents are **INFORMATIVE** elaboration.
- **Intent:** organize the scope, actions, methods, success criteria, enablers, evidence points, and trade-offs needed to achieve verification outcomes.
- **Relationship:** the strategy should evolve with the project and is a typical candidate for baselining under configuration management.
- **FRAMEWORK IMPLICATION:** a strategy record needs controlled rationale and links to actions, methods, criteria, enablers, evidence points, risks, and baselines. Exact field names remain framework design choices.

### 4.4 Define and perform procedures — 6.4.9.3(b)

- **Source classification:** procedure definition and performance are **NORMATIVE** under task-based conformance; detailed procedure contents and execution logic in NOTEs are **INFORMATIVE**.
- **Inputs:** verification strategy, actions, method, success criteria, expected results, object and environment constraints, enablers.
- **Tasks:** define procedures that support the actions; perform the procedures.
- **Outputs:** captured observations and measurements, comparison against expected results/criteria, determinations, and repeat work where anomaly resolution requires it.
- **FRAMEWORK IMPLICATION:** `Verification Case` can remain a framework-level logical intent object, while `Verification Procedure` is directly supported as the execution definition. The standard supports expected results and success criteria but does not define an `Oracle` entity.

The NOTE's execution logic can be abstracted, without treating the abstraction as a mandated notation:

`Perform procedure → Capture result → Compare with expected result → Apply success criteria → Determine correctness → Determine confidence`

### 4.5 Manage results — 6.4.9.3(c)

- **Source classification:** **NORMATIVE** tasks under task-based conformance; NOTE detail is **INFORMATIVE**.
- **Tasks:** record results and anomalies; record incidents/problems and track resolution; obtain approval-authority agreement that the object meets specified requirements; maintain traceability; provide key artefacts for baselines.
- **Outputs:** result and anomaly records, resolution status, approval record, trace links, and baseline candidates.
- **Relationships:** project assessment/control handles analysis and corrective action; quality assurance supports problem resolution; technical processes address changes; configuration management controls the baseline.
- **Traceability detail:** the NOTE explicitly describes bidirectional traceability between verified elements and the verification strategy, architecture, design, and requirements, including results, evidence, anomalies, deviations, and satisfaction status.
- **FRAMEWORK IMPLICATION:** `Requirement/Object ↔ Strategy/Action ↔ Procedure ↔ Result/Evidence ↔ Anomaly/Disposition ↔ Approval/Baseline` is evidence-backed as a relationship chain. The standard does not prescribe the repository's exact metamodel or closure state machine.

## 5. Verification versus Validation

Clause 6.4.11 applies a parallel prepare–perform–manage pattern to Validation, but changes the reference and decision context.

### 5.1 Validation purpose and outcomes — 6.4.11.1–6.4.11.2

- **Source classification:** **NORMATIVE**.
- **Intent:** obtain objective evidence that a system, when used, fulfils business or mission objectives, stakeholder needs and requirements, and intended use in the intended operational environment.
- **Normative objects:** system, stakeholders/intended users, intended use, business/mission objectives, stakeholder needs/requirements, operational environment, results, anomalies, and evidence.
- **Inputs/preconditions:** validation scope and reference basis, stakeholder context, representative environment, constraints, methods/criteria, and validated enablers.
- **Outcomes:** validation constraints and enablers are identified; the system is validated; corrective-action data and objective evidence are produced; results/anomalies are identified; traceability is established; stakeholders confirm intended use is fulfilled.
- **FRAMEWORK IMPLICATION:** validation claims require operational/contextual references and stakeholder confirmation that cannot be replaced by verification against specifications alone.

### 5.2 Prepare, perform, and manage validation — 6.4.11.3

- **Source classification:** tasks are **NORMATIVE** under task-based conformance; associated NOTE detail is **INFORMATIVE**.
- **Required activities:** identify scope/actions and constraints; select methods and success criteria; define a Validation Strategy; feed constraints/objectives to other technical processes; plan and obtain validated enablers; define and execute procedures; record results/anomalies and track problems; obtain stakeholder agreement; maintain traceability; provide key artefacts for baselines.
- **Outputs:** strategy, procedures, captured results/evidence, anomalies/problem status, stakeholder agreement, trace links, and baseline candidates.
- **Relationships:** project planning, assessment/control, risk, QA, configuration, requirements, architecture/design, and transition/operation processes provide or consume validation information.
- **FRAMEWORK IMPLICATION:** use a shared control pattern for V&V records, but type the reference basis, environment, actor/authority, and conclusion so validation cannot silently collapse into verification.

| Dimension | Verification — 6.4.9 | Validation — 6.4.11 |
|---|---|---|
| Primary question | Whether specified requirements and characteristics are fulfilled. | Whether the system in use fulfils business/mission objectives, stakeholder needs/requirements, and intended use. |
| Primary reference | Specified requirements, architecture, and design characteristics. | Intended use, business/mission objectives, stakeholder needs and requirements. |
| Object/context | System, system element, or artefact in a defined verification context. | System in the intended or representative operational environment, involving intended users or suitable surrogates. |
| Decision participants | Approval authority agrees the verified object meets specified requirements. | Stakeholders confirm and agree that intended use and needs are fulfilled. |
| Common structure | Scope/actions, constraints, methods, success criteria, strategy, enablers, procedures, results/anomalies, traceability, baseline inputs. | The same broad control structure, with validation-specific references and context. |

**FRAMEWORK IMPLICATION:** Verification and Validation records may share a common information backbone, but their claims, reference bases, environments, and approval roles must remain distinct. The slogans “built right” and “right system” are insufficient as definitions.

## 6. Project process support — Clauses 6.3.1–6.3.8

| Process | Clause-level contribution to verification | Framework implication | Classification |
|---|---|---|---|

### Configuration-to-reverification chain

The combined reading of 6.3.5 and 6.4.9 supports the following relationship as an **INTERPRETATION**:

`Configuration → Change → Impact evaluation → Verification or re-verification → Result/evidence → Approved baseline`

The standard supports repeated verification following anomaly resolution and verification/validation of approved changes, but it does not define a general `Regression` process or prescribe that all prior tests be rerun.

## 7. Information outputs — Annex B

Annex B is **INFORMATIVE** and its work-product examples are non-exhaustive. For Verification it identifies:

- a verified system as an artefact; and
- verification records/reports as information items that may contain the approach, criteria, results, and discrepancies.

**FRAMEWORK IMPLICATION:** the framework may define controlled Verification Strategy, Procedure, Result, Evidence, and Anomaly information items. It must not claim that ISO 15288 mandates those exact document titles or a single report format. ISO/IEC/IEEE 15289 is the high-priority follow-up source for life-cycle information-item content.

## 8. MBSE and model evidence — Annex D

Annex D is **INFORMATIVE**. It presents MBSE as formalized application of modeling across the life cycle, with models serving as a major knowledge source. It connects models to measurement, assessment/control, system analysis, decision management, Verification, and Validation. Model queries can support completeness and consistency checks, trade studies, measurement, reuse, dependency navigation, early interface/integration issue detection, and change-impact analysis.

Model analysis may act as a surrogate for some Verification or Validation when use of the actual system is infeasible, but confidence then depends on validation of the models and their intended use.

**FRAMEWORK IMPLICATION:** the progression `DBSE information model → MBSE representation → executable query/check → impact and evidence analysis` is supported as an informative direction. ISO 15288 does not mandate MBSE, SysML, a graph database, or any particular modeling tool.

| Q12 | What is the Assurance Case role? | It is an auditable claim–argument–evidence artefact that can organize verification evidence and expose coverage gaps; it is not a replacement for the Verification Process. | **NORMATIVE conceptual support + INTERPRETATION** |
| Q13 | How are anomaly, change, and re-verification handled? | Results/anomalies and problems are recorded/tracked; corrective action and controlled change can trigger repeat/re-verification through supporting processes. | **NORMATIVE + INFORMATIVE + INTERPRETATION** |
| Q14 | How does the baseline interact? | Key verification artefacts are supplied for baselines; Configuration Management identifies items, controls changes/status, audits configurations, and connects approved changes to V&V. | **NORMATIVE**, cross-process |
| Q15 | What does MBSE support? | Queries/checks, completeness/consistency, measurement, traceability, reuse, interface/integration analysis, impact analysis, and surrogate V&V when models are validated. | **INFORMATIVE** Annex D |

## 12. Meaningful normative gaps and follow-up sources

The following are genuine gaps relative to the proposed framework, not defects in ISO 15288:

- No universal Verification Coverage dimensions, completeness rule, or metric.
- No defined Verification Sufficiency decision model.
- No `Oracle`, `Verification Obligation`, or `Verification Case` entity.
- No general Verification independence requirement; only QA independence is explicit in the studied scope.
- No named Regression process or risk-based regression selection algorithm.
- No prescribed Verification Closure process, state model, or complete closure criteria.
- No mandated life-cycle document names or field-level schemas in Annex B.
- No mandated MBSE language, metamodel, query language, or tool qualification rule.

Prioritized follow-up research:

1. ISO/IEC/IEEE 15289 for life-cycle information-item content.
2. ISO/IEC/IEEE 24748-1 first, then other applicable parts, for life-cycle management and process-application guidance.
3. ARP4754B for aircraft/system development assurance, derived requirements, verification rigor, and independence.
4. ARP4761A for safety-assessment-derived rigor and evidence relationships.
5. DO-178C, DO-254, and DO-297 for item/domain-specific objectives, independence, coverage, configuration, and integration roles.
6. ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148 for requirement characteristics, needs-to-requirements transformation, and requirements information items; add after the initial cross-standard research round rather than interrupting it.
